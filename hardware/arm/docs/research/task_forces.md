# Task force / accuracy requirements for a low-cost hexapod arm (8-20 kg robot)

Compiled 2026-10-16. Sources are linked inline. Tags used:

- **[M]** measured value from a primary source (paper, thesis, or datasheet)
- **[S]** secondary source (review, press, retailer, or a figure quoted by another paper)
- **[E]** my engineering estimate or derivation (method shown). Treat as uncertain.
- **[GAP]** no good quantitative source found

"p95" means the 95th-percentile or worst-case design value. Where a paper gives one, it is used. Otherwise the value is an estimate and is marked [E].

---

## 0. Platform-level constraint that dominates several tasks (read first)

**A legged robot that is not anchored can push down only with its own weight.** An 8 kg hexapod weighs 78 N and a 20 kg one weighs 196 N. To keep the feet loaded and the robot stable, the usable downward tool force is about 0.6-0.8 x m g, so **~50-160 N** [E]. Several existing robots ran into this limit:

- A Husky-based soil sampler's 980 N linear actuator "managed to lift the robot off the ground" before it could reach 30 cm with a stalled auger. They fixed it with peck-drilling, not more force ([Masuo et al., IEEE RA-P 2026, OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3030892)) [M].
- MoistureMapper (AgileX Scout 2.0 with 2 x 3000 N actuators) could not fully insert its 15 cm TDR waveguides at many sites. "Excessively dense soil ... caused the front of the UGV to raise into the air" ([arXiv 2507.12716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.12716)) [M].
- USDA-ARS and Utah State University: the force needed to insert TDR rods "is often more than these small robots can generate" ([USDA ARS pub. 413410](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=413410)) [M].
- The VTT pasture weeding robot (Schunk LWA4P arm) "did not have enough strength to penetrate the tool to the ground in harder ground areas" at about 50 N ([Kotaniemi et al. 2024, arXiv 2405.12596](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12596)) [M].

Design implications [E]:

1. Do high-force axial work (pull-up, probe, stamp) close under the body. The hexapod's legs can supply the stroke by crouching or standing, so the arm does not have to. Put pushing forces directly through the body's center of mass.
2. Use rotation, augering, or vibration to cut axial thrust. Rotating penetrators cut axial force by up to ~50% (DEM) and by ~60-80% at a tangential/axial velocity ratio of about 20 in sand. Torque goes up in exchange ([escholarship 2cg9r2d6](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2cg9r2d6), [par.nsf.gov 10468701](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10468701), [arXiv 2503.07886](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07886v2)) [M]. One sand-simulant test went from about 600 N to about 100 N with rotation alone ([US patent 12680392](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12680392)) [S].
3. Use screw anchors or a foot-mounted anchor if you need more than about 150 N. Agrobot Lala anchors to 15 cm with about 80 mm screw anchors to react up to 720 N ([PMC9185546](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9185546/)) [M].
4. Tipping limit for a lateral tool force F applied at height h: F_max ≈ m g b / h, where b is the distance from the center of mass to the support-polygon edge. Example: 15 kg, b = 0.2 m, h = 0.3 m gives about 100 N [E].

Additional notes:

- **Root-breakage ceiling** [E]: eight herbaceous species had mean root tensile strengths of 12.0 MPa (_Setaria viridis_) to 26.5 MPa (_Pennisetum alopecuroides_), with _Trifolium repens_ at 22.0 MPa. These were mostly fine roots; strength drops as diameter grows ([Sci. Soil Water Conserv. abstract](https://journal12.magtechjournal.com/Jweb_stbc/EN/abstract/abstract9444.shtml)) [S]. Dandelion taproots are "generally up to 1/2 inch" in diameter and most lie 6-18 in deep ([Syngenta](https://www.syngenta.ca/pests/weed/dandelion/corn), [invasive.org](https://www.invasive.org/species/subject.cfm?sub=3887)) [S]. An 8 mm root at about 15 MPa breaks at roughly 750 N. In dry soil, though, the shoot and crown snap well before that ("in very dry soil the stem is more likely to break off near ground level", [US 5664819](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5664819)). A straight pull above about 150 N therefore usually leaves the root in the ground. Loosen the soil with a lever, fork, or core first.
- Soil moisture effect: roots extract most easily from very moist soil, while dry soil causes crown breakage (same patent) [S]. **[GAP]** I found no quantitative N-vs-moisture curve for weeds.
- Pull direction: vertical up. A lever or rock motion (the VTT tool mimics a manual three-finger "Grampa's"-style lever weeder under force control) lowers the peak force.
- Accuracy: VTT achieved ±5 mm tool-to-root (target ±10 mm) and ±20 mm weed localization [M].
- Orientation: top-down, ±10°.

### 1b. Stamping / stabbing (Deepfield / BoniRob)

- Tube stamp (Langsenkamp et al., CIGR 2014): 11 mm stamp, about 47 mm soil penetration, 70 W BLDC with a high-helix lead screw, two-spring tube that holds the weed, full cycle <600 ms. Field weed-control rate was 93.86% for weeds at BBCH ≤12. In the greenhouse, _Stellaria_ and _Capsella_ were 100% killed, _Setaria_ only 43% ([PDF](https://www.hs-osnabrueck.de/fileadmin/HSOS/Homepages/COALA/Veroeffentlichungen/2014-CIGR_2014_Tube_Stamp_for_mechanical_intra-row_individual_Plant_Weed_Control.pdf)) [M]. **No force is reported.**
- Deepfield high-speed stamp (Michaels, Haug & Albert, IROS 2015): about 1 cm stamp pushes weeds about 3 cm into soil, <100 ms per stamp (repeated hits for larger weeds). Peak throughput was 1.75 weeds/s at 43 weeds/m and 3.7 cm/s ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/bosch-deepfield-robotics-weed-control)) [S].
- **Stamp force estimate** [E]: F ≈ CI x tip area. An 11 mm tip has 95 mm². In a loose ridge or seedbed (CI 0.5-1 MPa) that gives 50-100 N; in firm soil (2 MPa) about 190 N. Add dynamic impact. Energy check: 100 N x 0.047 m ≈ 4.7 J per stroke, about 16 W at 0.3 s per stroke, which fits a 70 W motor. **Design: 100 N typical, 200 N p95, axial down.**
- Accuracy: the impact zone is 11 mm, so tool positioning needs about ±3 mm [E]. Odd.Bot Maverick (two delta arms with grippers that press and pull weeds) claims 2 mm accuracy and 2 weeds/s ([PMC12756371 review, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12756371/)) [S].

### 1c. Twisting / coring / chopping tools

- Dock robot (van Evert et al., JFR 2011): a single 0.20 m blade rotating at 1500 rpm about a vertical axis is pushed into the ground over the taproot (destroys it to 10-20 cm). It takes about 12 s per weed, the weeder was positioned within 0.1 m of the taproot in 73% of cases, and about 25% regrowth occurred ([WUR report](https://edepot.wur.nl/156334), [JFR abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/rob.20377)) [M/S]. It works poorly in dry or stony soil ([Weed Res. 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/wre.12407)) [S].
- Shovel-tooth plant remover (rape breeding, yellow clay, 23% moisture): 34.8 N mean soil penetration force with two thin teeth at 8 cm depth. **763 N gathering (squeeze) force** was needed to hold the root-soil clod, with a 94% removal rate ([Xing et al., PLoS One 2023, PMC10721175](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10721175/)) [M]. Lesson: thin blades go in easily, but clamping the soil around a root is very force-hungry.
- **Coring/twist tool torque estimate** [E]: a 25 mm OD tube, 80 mm deep, with soil shear strength 20-60 kPa needs about 1.5-5 N·m plus about 50-150 N axial. Rotation lowers the axial force (see section 0).

### 1d. Electrical zapping

- Commercial machines: Zasso XP300, phased DC, up to 7000 V. RootWave Pro, HF AC, up to 5000 V, 2 s per treatment ([ResearchGate figure caption](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Picture-of-the-Zasso-XP300-Xpower-left-and-Rootwave-Pro-right-electric-weeding_fig1_369342641)) [S]. A prototype study estimated about 250 J per weed (J. Agric. Machinery 2023, [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/4836a1bbc99c41c7b554c5800643c11f)) [S].
- Low-energy pulsed microshock with a flat-plate electrode (Bloomer et al., _Agronomy_ 2023, 13:2694): broadleaf seedlings were 100% killed at 0.1-0.9 MJ/ha at 5 plants/m², which is **about 2-18 J per plant** [E, derived from the reported figures]. _Lolium_ was only about 50% killed ([abstract, DOI 10.3390/agronomy13112694](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy13112694)) [M]. In the field it gave >90% control at 15 kJ/ha for broadleaves (about 0.3 J per plant) and 363-555 kJ/ha for ryegrass (7-11 J per plant) ([Bloomer et al., Sustainability 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/su16114324)) [M].
- **Force**: contact only. Pressing the plant to the soil raised the discharged energy (Bloomer 2023). Light press of about 2-10 N [E].
- Arm implication: 5-7 kV isolation on the end effector, and the return path goes through the soil and the robot's feet [E]. Accuracy ±10 mm is fine for a plate electrode [E].

Hexapod spot-spray hardware:

- **Force at the tool**: about 0 N (jet reaction is <0.1 N) [E].
- **Tool mass**: a micro solenoid valve plus nozzle, about 10-150 g. Put the pump and tank on the body. A 12 V diaphragm pump at 3.5-5 L/min weighs about 0.3-0.66 kg ([retailer listings](https://www.manomano.co.uk/p/micro-diaphragm-pump-12v-60w-electric-pump-made-of-technical-plastic-silent-240528831)) [S]. For micro-dosing a peristaltic or micro-gear pump of about 0.1-0.3 kg is enough [E].
- **Stand-off** [E]: DoD jets work at about 2-10 cm. Flat-fan spot nozzles work at 20-50 cm, and the spot diameter grows with height (110° fan at 25 cm gives about 70 cm swath width). For per-weed spots of 2-6 cm, use 3-15 cm stand-off with a narrow-angle or solid-stream nozzle.
- **Accuracy**: ±2.5-5 mm for single-seedling micro-dosing; ±10-30 mm for spot spraying.
- **Orientation**: top-down ±15°.
- **Dose per weed** [E]: micro-dose 1-25 µL (5-25 µg glyphosate). Conventional spot spray is about 0.5-5 mL per plant.

## 3. Seeding and planting plugs

- **Plug extraction from tray** (Jiang et al., PLoS One 2017, 72-cell 38.2 mL cells): plug-to-cell adhesion 1.41-3.09 N. Needle extrusion/clamp force 9.45-18.67 N, mean 13.4-17.4 N depending on 78-82% moisture. Four 2 mm needles inserted 40 mm, 0.44 MPa cylinder, **2.8 s per seedling**, 100% success ([PMC5498097](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5498097/)) [M]. **Design: 20 N grip / 5 N pull.**
- Seedling handling limits: rush seedlings have a grasp limit of about 4 N (bio-yield) and stems buckle at about 1.9 N, so push the plug, not the stem ([JIRCAS JARQ 38](https://www.jircas.go.jp/sites/default/files/publication/jarq/38-01-06_0.pdf)) [S].
- **Planting-hole (dibble) force** [E]: F ≈ CI x tip area + friction. A 20-25 mm dibble 50-80 mm deep at CI 0.5-1 MPa (tilled bed) needs about 150-500 N, and pasture or untilled soil (1.5-3 MPa) needs 500-1500 N. **That is beyond a hexapod's weight.** Use a rotary auger or drill (see section 0) or plant into pre-loosened soil.
- **Seed placement**: a 5-10 mm dibble or press wheel 10-30 mm deep in a seedbed needs about 10-80 N [E]. FarmBot uses a vacuum needle seed injector with a "millimeter accuracy" claim ([FarmBot docs](https://genesis.farm.bot/v1.3/FarmBot-Genesis-V1.3/tools/seed-injector)) [S]. FarmDroid claims <10 mm seed placement with RTK ([Cornish Mutual](https://www.cornishmutual.co.uk/news/the-autonomous-solar-powered-seeding-and-weeding-robot)) [S].
- **Masses** [E]: seeds <1 g; a wet plug of 30-50 g (7-10 g dry substrate per cell at about 80% moisture in Jiang 2017).
- **Accuracy**: ±5-10 mm. **Orientation**: top-down vertical.

## 4. Soil coring / probing and soil-moisture probe insertion

### 4a. Soil strength reference values

- ASABE S313.3 cone: 30°, base 20.27 mm (323 mm², soft soils) or 12.83 mm (130 mm², hard soils), pushed at 30 mm/s ([summary](https://agro.icm.edu.pl/agro/element/bwmeta1.element.agro-bff75314-adee-46e7-a86f-f4c1b0a59579/c/5-17.pdf), [ASABE](https://elibrary.asabe.org/abstract.asp?aid=44232)) [S].
- Root-limiting CI is **2 MPa** (Taylor & Gardner 1963), with 3 MPa as "critical" ([ARS cswq-0436](https://ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/50701000/cswq-0436-jung.pdf), [Scielo](https://scielo.br/j/rceres/a/M3hMNGvdywfGHv7pdhzNnzz/?lang=en)) [S]. The UW Extension compaction flag is 300 psi (2.07 MPa), measured at field capacity or slightly drier ([UW A4144](https://stcroix.extension.wisc.edu/files/2018/10/A4144-Cone-Penetrometer-Use.pdf)) [M].
- Pasture examples: 3.0-3.9 MPa at 0-20 cm in a Cerrado pasture Oxisol at 13-14% moisture ([UFV](https://periodicos.ufv.br/rbas/article/view/14002)) [M]. In Paraíba, grazed pasture had a maximum mean of 2.69 MPa versus 1.92 MPa for cultivated land ([UFPB](https://repositorio.ufpb.br/jspui/handle/123456789/3949)) [M]. Trampling makes the 0-10 cm layer the hardest in grazed land.
- **Working values** [E]: tilled or moist soil 0.3-1 MPa; typical field or pasture at field capacity 1-2 MPa; dry or compacted pasture 3-5 MPa or more.

### 4b. Force to insert a 10-20 mm probe 50-150 mm deep [E]

Tip force = CI x A. Add shaft friction of roughly 10-30 kPa adhesion/friction x lateral area (π D L). An open sampling tube also has internal core friction, which is not included here.

| Probe Ø | Tip area | Tip @1 MPa | @2 MPa | @3 MPa | @5 MPa | Shaft friction, 150 mm deep (10-30 kPa) | Typical total (moist, 1-2 MPa) | p95 (dry/compacted) |
| ------- | -------- | ---------- | ------ | ------ | ------ | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------- |
| 10 mm   | 79 mm²   | 79 N       | 157 N  | 236 N  | 393 N  | 47-141 N                                | **130-300 N**                  | ~550 N              |
| 15 mm   | 177 mm²  | 177 N      | 353 N  | 530 N  | 884 N  | 71-212 N                                | **250-560 N**                  | ~1100 N             |
| 20 mm   | 314 mm²  | 314 N      | 628 N  | 942 N  | 1571 N | 94-283 N                                | **400-900 N**                  | ~1850 N             |

For 50 mm depth, scale the shaft term by 1/3; the tip term is roughly the same. **Every row except a 10 mm probe in moist soil exceeds a 8-20 kg hexapod's ~50-160 N push.** A thin-walled open corer (e.g., 19 mm OD / 16 mm ID) has about 80 mm² of edge area, which helps, but core friction adds force.

Existing robots and tools:

- Hand probes: AMS 7/8" OD probe takes a 3/4" x 12" core; the 1-1/8" probe takes a 1" core ([AMS](https://ams-samplers.com/1-1-8-x-33-ez-kleen-soil-probe), [Gemplers](https://gemplers.com/products/ams-33l-collapsible-soil-probe)) [M]. People push them with body weight (about 300-800 N) [E].
- Sampling practice: 15-20 cores per composite sample. Pasture depth is 4 in (UK), 4-6 in (MSU, UF), or 6-7 in (Missouri) ([UK AGR-252](https://forages.ca.uky.edu/sites/forages.ca.uky.edu/files/agr252.pdf), [MSU P3858](https://extension.msstate.edu/sites/default/files/publications/P3858_web.pdf), [MU G9215](https://extension.missouri.edu/media/wysiwyg/Extensiondata/Pub/pdf/agguides/soils/g09215.pdf)) [M].
- Robot penetrometer on Thorvald: 600 N max push, 1100 N load cell, 2.5 cm/s to 50 cm, about 50 s per location ([Fentanes et al., arXiv 1803.08069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.08069)) [M].
- Agrobot Lala (Husky): up to 720 N, 30 cm depth, rotating bore-tipped extractor at 1 mm/s, screw anchors to 15 cm, samples of 23-46 g, about 30 min per sample including on-board nitrate analysis ([PMC9185546](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9185546/)) [M].
- ORNL Husky sampler: 76 mm Ryobi auger on a 980 N Firgelli actuator, peck drilling, 30 cm sample in 2 min ([OSTI 3030892](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3030892)) [M].
- **Sample mass** [E]: 19 mm x 150 mm core ≈ 43 cm³ x 1.2-1.5 g/cm³ ≈ 50-65 g (+ water).
- **Accuracy**: ±20-50 mm horizontal; verticality ±5°. **Orientation**: top-down.

### 4c. Soil-moisture probe insertion [E, no measured N found]

- TEROS 12: 3 needles, 5.5 cm long, about 3.2 mm diameter ([METER specs](https://metergroup.com/products/teros-12/teros-12-tech-specs/), [HOBO RXW-T12](https://assets.testequity.com/te1/Documents/pdf/hobo/HOBO-by-Onset_RXW-T12-900_Sensor_Data-Sheet_0124.pdf)) [M]. Estimate: tips 24 mm² x 2 MPa ≈ 50 N, plus friction 17-50 N, so **about 70-100 N** (p95 about 150 N in dry soil).
- FieldScout TDR 150: 2 rods, 5 mm diameter, 38/75/120/200 mm long ([manual](https://acl.design.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Product-Manual-FieldScout-TDR150-Item-_6445.pdf), [Tempcon](https://www.tempcon.co.uk/tdr-rods-64xx)) [M]. Estimate for 75 mm rods: about 80-150 N. For 120 mm rods: **about 120-250 N** (p95 about 400 N).
- A UAV payload with a 5 cm TDR probe succeeded >85% of the time when insertion exceeded 4.5 cm. Hard, dry surfaces were the main failure mode ([COMPAG 2024 S0168169924009098](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169924009098)) [S]. A waveform correction tolerates partial insertion if ≥50% of the rod is in the soil ([ARS 413410](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=413410)) [M].
- **Recommendation**: choose short-needle (≤6 cm) sensors and insert using body weight. Add force sensing so insertion stops when it stalls, and log the insertion depth.

## 5. Leaf / tissue sampling

- UCR leaf-retrieval end effector (avocado, citrus): enclosing cutter with a **20 N stem-cutting design target**. The end effector is 1.09 kg on a 2.6 kg-payload arm. Leaves were 24-86 mm wide and 54-150 mm long. Localization error was 8-16 mm mean, which the enclosing geometry tolerated. 69.2% capture, 77.8% cut ([arXiv 2208.05032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05032)) [M].
- Alternatives: corn-leaf gripper with a heated nichrome-wire cutter ([US 12520772](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12520772)) [S]; T-REX microneedle tissue gripper, 66.6% grasp success on artificial plants ([arXiv 2505.01654](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01654)) [M].
- **Forces** [E]: leaf grip 2-10 N; petiole tear-off by pull 5-20 N; cut 5-20 N.
- **Payload**: 10-50 g of tissue.
- **Accuracy**: ±10-15 mm with an enclosing end effector.
- **Orientation**: side or angled approach that follows the leaf normal.

## 6. Water sampling

- Sample volumes in existing systems: 45 mL syringe sampler; 150 mL drone bailer; up to 5 x 500 mL per UAV flight; 2 x 1 L on the NORDACC surface vehicle; Speedip V2 is 2 L / about 2.7 kg with water ([PMC10387611](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10387611/), [search summary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42452-025-06544-x)) [M/S].
- **Payload** [E]: 100-500 mL is typical for field kits, so 0.12-0.55 kg including the bottle. Or keep the bottle on the body and use only a tube plus a peristaltic pump on the arm (about 0.05 kg at the tip).
- **Force**: weight plus drag, under 10 N.
- **Hard part**: reach down a bank of 0.3-1 m to the water. Use a weighted tube or a dip bottle on a tether rather than a long arm [E].

Soft gripper guidance:

- Keep normal grip force at or below **5 N** (cherry tomato, blueberry), **10 N** (strawberry body, round tomato), or **24 N** (apple).
- A force-controlled or compliant gripper (Fin-Ray, TPU) with current-based torque control is enough. The cherry-tomato gripper used a Dynamixel XM430 in current mode [M].
- Ripeness changes tomato stiffness several-fold (about 2,000-10,000 N/m), so limit strain rather than force ([arXiv 2608.23983](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23983)) [S].

Accuracy and orientation:

- Strawberry peduncle cut needs ±3-5 mm [E]. Robofruit traced 26% of its failures to position error [M].
- Tomato and apple with compliant or vacuum grippers need ±10 mm [E]. Blueberry needs ±5-15 mm (CLASP).
- Orientation: side approach for trellised or bush fruit. For ground strawberries, top-down or angled at 30-60°.

## 9. Pruning small stems (3-10 mm)

- **Grapevine (Concord) canes 5-11 mm**: on average **320 N at the cutting point for an 8 mm cane** with bypass shears. Force is roughly linear in diameter (R² = 0.74), and dead canes are harder. The Bumblebee end effector is a 0.45 kg bypass shear driven by an **8 N·m servo** through a mechanical-advantage-3 pulley with 200 lb line ([arXiv 2112.00291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.00291)) [M].
- **Apple branches 6-20 mm** (bypass/shear pruner with torque sensor): maximum **6.98 N·m** at 20 mm ('Fuji', straight cut, branch at blade center). Placing the branch near the pivot lowers torque significantly. Honeycrisp needs the least, Gala the most ([Zahid et al., HortTech 32:90, 2022](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/an-apple-tree-branch-pruning-analysis/)) [M]. A 3R end effector cut branches up to about 25 mm ([Zahid et al., COMPAG 2020](https://projects.sare.org/media/pdf/1/s/2/1-s2.0-S0168169920323139-main.pdf)) [M].
- **Scaling** [E]: from the 320 N / 8 mm point and a linear trend, 3 mm needs about 100-150 N, 5 mm about 200 N, and 10 mm about 400-500 N at the cutting point, with dry or dead wood up to about 1.5x. With the stem 15-25 mm from the pivot, pivot torque is **about 2 N·m (3 mm) to 8-12 N·m (10 mm)**.
- **Herbaceous stems**: strawberry peduncles (about 2 mm) cut at ≤7 N; leaf petioles about 20 N (sections 5 and 8).
- **Design**: put a geared shear in the end effector, about 10 N·m at the pivot or 400-500 N at the blade root, so the arm only carries reaction forces near zero. **Accuracy** ±5 mm. **Orientation**: blade plane perpendicular to the stem, any approach.

Takeaway [E]:

- Field robots cluster at **IP54 (minimum) to IP65/67 (target)**.
- **IP65** keeps out dust and water jets, so the robot can be hosed down after herbicide or mud. **IP67** adds puddle immersion and is worth having for feet, lower legs and any end effector that touches soil or water.
- A realistic low-cost target is **IP54 for the arm joints**, plus **IP65-67 for the tool and wrist** with a sacrificial boot.
- Operating range of **−10 to +45 °C**, plus solar-load margin (dark housings in sun reach 60-70 °C; mark as uncertain).
- Herbicides (glyphosate salts) and fertilizers are corrosive. Use stainless or anodized parts and sealed connectors.

### Actuator-sizing guidance from this table [E]

- **Arm (positioning and light work)**: 1 kg payload at 0.5 m reach plus a 20-30 N continuous tip force. Shoulder torque is about 10-15 N·m static and 20-30 N·m peak.
- **High axial force (100-200 N)**: deliver it near the body (r ≤ 0.2-0.25 m) and preferably through body or leg motion. That is 20-50 N·m at the shoulder if the arm must react it.
- **Above about 150 N (soil cores, dibbles in firm soil)**: use rotation, augering, or anchoring. Do not size the arm for it.
- **Wrist**: about 0.5 N·m for fruit twisting. Handle 5-10 N·m valve turns with a dedicated tool or by yawing the body.
- **Gripper**: compliant and force-controlled. Clamp 0-30 N with closed-loop limits of 5, 10 and 24 N for fruit classes.
- **Shear tool**: self-contained, about 10 N·m at the pivot or about 400-500 N at the blade.
