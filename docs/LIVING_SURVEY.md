# The Living Survey

A measured site that moves under a wind setting without its measurement ever changing.

Today that is **one procedural tree**. The pipeline is real — a skeleton extractor scored against
ground truth, a motion model with proven bounds, a runtime that rewrites a Gaussian splat's
positions on the GPU every frame — but the only tree that moves is `data/tiles/synthetic-tree`, a
12,000-splat fixture generated by `tools/captures/synthetic_tree.py`. The skeleton extractor has never been run on a
real capture. What that would take, and why it did not happen here, is in
[CAPTURES.md](CAPTURES.md#the-real-tree-what-was-tried-and-what-it-would-take).

Read this page for the mechanism and its limits. The numbers below were all measured; where the
measurement was taken under software GL (SwiftShader), it says so, because those numbers are not
representative of a machine with a GPU.

### What this proves, and what it does not

The rest of this page is long, and the mechanism travels further than the caveats when it is
quoted. So both halves, up front:

**Proven, by measurement or by construction:**

- The canonical positions are never written to. A 1000-frame test asserts byte-identity on the
  engine's array, the fixture, the deformer's own copy and the cached bake matrix.
- Calm restores the measured pose **exactly**, not approximately — `bakeResidualM` measured 0
  against the real engine, so there is no epsilon anywhere in that claim.
- A splat tileset's positions can be rewritten every frame from outside the engine, with no fork
  and no readback, and the write lands in the frame it was made in.
- Motion cannot contaminate a measurement, by mechanism rather than by care.
- Idle stays idle: at calm the scene requests zero renders.

**Not proven, and not provable from here:**

- **That any of this works on a real scan.** The only tree that moves is invented. The skeleton
  extractor has been scored against ground truth but has never seen a capture.
- **That the sway reads as _that_ tree** rather than as generic foliage motion — the thing the
  Living Survey actually claims. No test here can answer it.
- **That the stale draw order is acceptable at a usable wind strength.** The threshold constant is
  a hypothesis, and `DEFAULT_WIND_STRENGTH` was derived from it rather than from observation.
- **What any of it costs on a GPU.** Every frame-rate figure here is software rasterisation.

The first three need a person, a real capture and real hardware. Until then this is a mechanism
that works on a tree nobody surveyed.

## Measured, simulated, fixture

The whole feature turns on a viewer never confusing these three, so they are named first.

| Thing                                    | What it is   | Where that is stated                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A real capture's splat positions         | Measured     | site provenance: capture date, GSD, license, attribution                |
| `data/tiles/synthetic-tree`              | **Fixture**  | "No capture date or resolution recorded" + the manifest's own words     |
| The skeleton rig (nodes, parents, radii) | Derived      | `rig.sourceNote`, printed verbatim in the Inspector                     |
| Per-node stiffness                       | **Invented** | a plausible gradient interpolated from constants; no biomechanics       |
| Wind `strength`                          | **Invented** | dimensionless 0..1, labelled "an arbitrary scale, not a wind speed"     |
| The sway itself                          | Simulated    | the ambient **Simulated motion** badge, plus the Inspector's Motion row |
| Displacement in metres, sort staleness   | Computed     | proven bounds over the rig, not samples                                 |

The Inspector splits one site into **Geometry** (what the catalog records: capture date, ground
sample distance, and the pipeline note verbatim) and **Motion** (Simulated, with the rig's source
note). "Measured capture" is earned, not assumed — it is said only when the catalog holds a date or
a resolution, and the synthetic tree holds neither, so it reads "No capture date or resolution
recorded · procedural; no capture, no reconstruction". The badge deliberately says nothing about
the geometry at all: it cannot see whether a given site was surveyed or invented, and for the only
tree that currently moves the answer is "invented".

Nothing about the motion can contaminate a measurement, and that is mechanism rather than care:
Gaussian splats never write depth and the splat primitive early-returns on the pick pass, so
`scene.sampleHeight` does not see them (measured: `undefined` with the globe hidden), a click
passes straight through a swaying tree to the terrain behind it, and no measurement tool can pick
one. The same property is why the badge has to be ambient — there is no hover or selection to hang
a disclosure off.

## Canonical and ephemeral, as mechanism

The product principle is that a living world never drifts away from its survey. That is enforced
by the shape of the code, not by a comment asserting it.

At attach, `SplatDeformer` decodes the baked positions once into buffers it owns and never writes
to again. Every tick computes

```text
displaced = f(canonical, t, wind)
```

from that pristine copy and uploads the result. There is no read-modify-write anywhere in the
path: the previous frame's output is never an input. So error cannot accumulate however long the
wind blows, and `strength = 0` does not "settle back" — it recomputes the canonical pose and
writes exactly that.

Two measurements make the claim exact rather than approximate:

- **`bakeResidualM` is 0.** The deformer re-bakes an undisplaced splat with the same term order
  `GaussianSplatPrimitive.transformTile` uses, and compares against the engine's own baked
  positions. Against the real engine in a browser (`apps/web/e2e/livingSurvey.spec.ts`) the largest
  disagreement over 12,000 splats is exactly zero, so wind → 0 restores the measured bytes with no
  epsilon in the claim. A residual above `BAKE_RESIDUAL_LIMIT_M` is a refusal, not a warning.
- **A 1000-frame immutability test.** `apps/web/src/__tests__/splatDeformer.test.ts` drives 1000
  frames of varying wind and then asserts byte-identity on `primitive._positions`, the harness's
  canonical array, the deformer's own canonical copy and the engine's cached bake matrix — and that
  `primitive._positions` is still the same object, not an equal one. After all that, zero wind
  lands on the engine's exact float32 bits again, word for word.

`tile.content.positions`, `primitive._positions`, `snapshot.positions` and the glTF POSITION array
are read-only to this feature, forever. `transformTile` rewrites the first of those in place from
the pristine glTF attribute, so a write there would make a deformation permanent and compound it
across every rebuild — the measured geometry would be destroyed silently, with nothing on screen
to show it. The only write targets are our staging buffer and the GPU texture.

## How it works

### The attribute texture

From `Shaders/PrimitiveGaussianSplatVS.glsl` (CesiumJS 1.145), each splat owns two adjacent
RGBA32UI texels on one row:

```glsl
ivec2 posCoord = ivec2(int((texIdx & rowMask) << 1), int(texIdx >> rowShift));
ivec2 covCoord = ivec2(int(((texIdx & rowMask) << 1) | 1u), int(texIdx >> rowShift));
```

The even column holds the position as raw float32 bits (`uintBitsToFloat`); the odd column beside
it holds packed covariance halves and an RGBA8 colour. Displacing a splat is writing three
float32s. Covariance and colour are never touched, which is what makes this safe without
reimplementing the engine's packer.

`rowMask` and `rowShift` are read from the primitive, never recomputed from an assumed maximum
texture size. They differ per machine — 8192 here under SwiftShader against 16384 on a typical
desktop GPU — and a wrong guess addresses every splat into the wrong texel while still producing a
picture.

### Why `CustomShader` is not the mechanism

`tileset.customShader` does not reach splat content in 1.145. Splats bypass the Model pipeline
entirely: `Scene/GaussianSplatPrimitive.js` builds its own render resources, its own
`PrimitiveGaussianSplatVS/FS` and its own DrawCommand, with zero `customShader` references. The
obvious route is closed. See [ADR 0006](DECISIONS/0006-splat-texture-rewrite.md).

### Mechanism (C′): interception, no patch, no readback

`Texture.copyFrom` takes a contiguous rectangle, and each position texel sits next to its
covariance texel, so any rectangle covering the positions covers the covariance too — writing rows
means supplying those bytes as well. Rather than reimplementing the packer (a lane-order mistake
corrupts geometry and colour silently) or reading the texture back (`gl.readPixels`, measured
762–898 ms for the first readback), `splatCapture.ts` keeps the buffer the engine already packed.

`generateSplatTexture` resolves `generateFromAttributes` as a property lookup on the
`GaussianSplatTextureGenerator` module object at call time, and that module is exported from the
public barrel (`cesium/Source/Cesium.js:638`). Replacing the property hands us the exact
`Uint32Array` the engine goes on to upload — correct by construction, and free. **No engine patch
was written**; `patches/@cesium__engine@26.3.0.patch` is the unrelated terrain-fill guard and is
untouched.

Cross-validated three ways on the `mygla` capture (57,410 splats): captured buffer → decoded
positions against `primitive._positions`, max absolute error **0.0 m**; GPU readback against
`_positions`, **0.0 m**; captured against readback, **0 differing words of 466,944**.

The interception only sees calls made after it is installed, so `CesiumSceneManager` installs it at
construction, before any tile loads. A snapshot it missed reports `no-capture` and the tree stays
still. `gl.readPixels` remains the recovery path for that case and is deliberately not implemented
on the steady-state path.

### The un-bake, and telling "re-derive" from "refuse"

`transformTile` writes `content.positions[i] = B · gltfPositions[i]`, where
`B = inverse(rootTransform) · tile.computedTransform · axisCorrection · content.worldTransform`,
and caches `B` on the content. Everything downstream is in that baked frame, while the rig is in
the local ENU frame the capture was authored in.

`rig.canonicalChecksum` is FNV-1a over the raw position bytes — **bit-exact, not tolerant** — and
was taken over the local positions, which `B` has since destroyed. The deformer therefore un-bakes
by `B⁻¹` before checksumming. That works because the capture pipeline snaps every coordinate to
SPZ's 1/4096 m grid before writing: `snap(B⁻¹ · B · p)` recovers the original float32 exactly as
long as the round trip's error stays well inside half a grid step, and it is around 2e-6 m for a
tree-sized capture against a 1.2e-4 m half-step. The synthetic tree's digest survives tiling,
loading and baking as `fnv1a32:12000:8b008bc0`, asserted in e2e.

This is also the discriminator the plan left open. `SiteManager.clampToGround` sets
`tileset.modelMatrix` after an asynchronous terrain sample, so every site rebuilds its snapshot
seconds after load with every baked position changed. A bit-exact checksum over the _baked_
positions could not tell that apart from the wrong tileset. Over the _un-baked_ positions it can:
same digest under a new `B` means clamp-to-ground moved the model, so the assignment is re-derived
from the new base (`rederivations` counts it); a different digest means these are not the splats
the rig was built for, and the deformer refuses. Rebuilds re-derive; they never re-apply the old
displacement.

### Refusals

Before its first write the deformer checks that the tileset is single-tile, that the root
transform's Z column is the geodetic normal (measured at 1.0 to 15 significant figures on a real
capture), that the tree's own points stand along local +Z, and that the checksum matches. On any
failure it writes nothing, ever, and reports which: `no-primitive`, `no-snapshot`, `no-texture`,
`no-bake-transform`, `no-capture`, `multi-tile`, `layout`, `frame`, `upright`, `checksum`, `bake`,
`internal`. A tree that does not move is honest; a tree whose wrong splats move is not.

### Per frame

`LivingSurveyManager` ticks on `scene.preUpdate` (which fires every tick even in
`requestRenderMode`, unlike `preRender`), computes node transforms with `deform()` from
`@twin/world`, and hands them to each attached deformer. Time is scene time —
`viewer.clock.currentTime` against a fixed epoch — so the same clock value always produces the same
frame, on any machine.

Because the frame is a pure function of that clock, a clock that has stopped and a motion model with
no motion in it produce the identical symptom — a tree that bends once and then sits there — and the
two were confused once already. Two guards now keep them apart: `LivingSurveyManager` logs a warning
when scene time repeats for two seconds of ticks while wind is on, and an e2e test asserts that the
real viewer's clock advances under `requestRenderMode`. It does; the model was the culprit.

`scene.requestRender()` is called only while some deformer holds displaced positions, plus the one
frame that restores the measurement. At calm the deformer writes nothing at all, because
`markMovingNodes` compares against the identity transform _exactly_ and `@twin/world` guarantees
bit-exact identity at zero wind. An idle scene stays idle; a tolerance anywhere upstream would
quietly turn a still survey into a scene that renders forever.

Uploads cover only the rows the moving nodes occupy. For the 12,000-splat tree on this machine that
is three rows of 8192 texels: 98,304 words, 384 KB per frame. (S0 measured whole-texture upload of
1.875 MB at ~1.2 ms CPU plus ~0.2 ms GPU submit — under SwiftShader, so treat it as an order of
magnitude, not a budget.)

### Animation and the quality ladder

`PerformanceManager` assumed a still camera meant a still scene: it only collected ladder evidence
while moving, and `sharpenAtRest` restored full device-pixel-ratio and MSAA 500 ms after the camera
stopped. With wind on, every animated frame would have rendered in the most expensive configuration
available, forever, while the ladder stayed blind to the cost.

`setAnimating(boolean)` fixes both. While animating, the still frame is not sharpened, and turning
it on drops an already-sharpened frame back to the ladder's step at once, the way the first frame of
a gesture does. Animated frames count against quality and never for it (`frameWeight`, pure and
tested): they feed the slow/good accounting that steps the ladder **down** — smooth ones included,
so one hitch a minute cannot ratchet quality away for good — but they never supply the credit that
steps it back **up**, and they are never the before/after sample that judges whether a step helped.
An animation can be smooth on a machine that cannot pan, and raising cost on the strength of frames
that are cheap because nothing is being asked of the machine is how an oscillation starts. An
animating scene can coarsen and earns its quality back on the next gesture. `animating` is surfaced
in the performance snapshot so the developer panel's `fps` is not read as something it is not. With
`setAnimating(false)` — the default — every prior test passes unmodified.

## How the tree moves: resonant modes, a gust field, and per-splat flutter

Three layers, each answering a defect the one before it could not:

| layer                                         | what it moves                                            | added in |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| damped resonant modes under turbulent forcing | each rig node, in its own plane and at its own frequency | S8       |
| a travelling gust field                       | the load each node sees, retarded by where it stands     | S9       |
| per-splat flutter                             | each splat, on top of its node                           | S9       |

The first version of this model was **quasi-static**. It computed where a tree would come to
_rest_ under a steady wind: a bend proportional to the gust magnitude, every node about one shared
axis, modulated by a slow noise term. Measured on the synthetic tree at the default wind, its
highest tip moved **0.149 mm per frame** — 0.02 px on a 6 m tree filling 800 px — and its wind
vector went from 0.084 at `t = 0` to 0.086 five seconds later. It was a correct load model and it
was not motion. A human on a GPU reported exactly what the arithmetic says: the tree bends, and
then it is a photograph.

Three things were structurally wrong, and none of them was a tuning problem:

1. **70 % of the wind was a constant** (`WIND_BASE`), so the tree snapped to a steady bend and the
   remaining 30 % gusted at 0.22 noise-units per second — a five-to-fifteen-second cycle.
2. **Every node shared one bending axis**, `up × downwind`, computed once for the whole rig. Nodes
   differed only in amplitude, so the crown could only ever move as a single flat sheet.
3. **Nothing had a natural frequency.** There was no restoring force, no damping, no oscillator.
   The model had essentially no energy above 1 Hz, where a real 6 m tree rings at 0.5–1.5 Hz, its
   branches at 1–3 Hz and its leaf clusters at 5–20 Hz.

What replaced it, in `packages/world/src/modes.ts` and `deform.ts`:

**The forcing is band-limited turbulence**, written as a fixed sum of nine sinusoids between
0.4 Hz and 10 Hz, with amplitudes falling as `f^-1/2` — the Kolmogorov family, flattened, because
over this narrow a band the textbook `f^-5/6` put the tip's dominant response near 0.5 Hz — and
phases drawn once from a hash. The amplitudes sum to exactly 1, which is what keeps every bound
below closed-form.

**Every node is a damped oscillator** and gets the analytic steady-state response to that forcing:

```
θ_i(t) = ℓ_i · [ lean_i(t) + Σ_k A_k · H_i(ω_k) · sin(ω_k·(t − τ_i) + φ_k + ψ_i(ω_k)) ]
H_i(ω) = 1 / √((1 − (ω/ω_i)²)² + (2·ζ_i·ω/ω_i)²)
ψ_i(ω) = −atan2(2·ζ_i·ω/ω_i, 1 − (ω/ω_i)²)
```

A node amplifies what lands near its own `ω_i` and ignores the rest, so the trunk picks up the slow
energy and a twig picks up the fast, without anything being told which is which.

**`ℓ_i` is the node's own segment length**, and it is there so that a joint is a _curvature_ rather
than a bend. Without it, refining a rig makes the tree floppier for free: subdividing the fixture's
trunk from eight joints to ten raised the worst-case displacement bound by 48 % for a tree of
exactly the same shape. On a fixture that is a curiosity; on a rig out of `skeleton.py`, whose node
spacing is whatever the extractor happened to produce and is uneven along a single limb, it would
make stiffness a property of the sampling rather than of the tree.

**`τ_i` is a real convection delay**, not a hash — see the wind field below.

**`ω_i` comes from geometry, never from the band label.** A cantilever's fundamental goes as
`radius / length²`, and `length` here is the path from the node to the furthest tip it carries —
both measurable on any rig, where "this is a branch" is an opinion that `skeleton.py` will be
guessing at in the field. The exponents are beam theory; the scale constant is calibration, and
says so in the code. It was refitted in S9: the old value of 220 was chosen against a fixture whose
trunk was 70 cm across, and `r / L²` for a post is several times what it is for a tree, so the
constant that cancelled it was several times too small and every other member of the rig inherited
the error. At 450 the fixture's trunk rings at 1.33 Hz, its primary limbs at 3–8 Hz and its leaf
clusters at a median 10.7 Hz. Damping `ζ` runs 0.05–0.15 from thickness — trees are lightly damped,
which is what makes them ring.

**Each node sways in its own plane.** Mostly downwind, swung by a per-node azimuth of up to ±0.7 rad
and tilted out of horizontal by a per-node twist, both hashed from the node's id. The _steady lean_
stays exactly downwind for every node, because that is what steady drag is; only the oscillation
gets a plane of its own. That split is what stops the crown being a sheet while keeping a branch
from acquiring a permanent sideways set.

**The steady lean is the minority partner**: 30 % of the load, and 35 % of each node's angular
budget. It is kept because a tree in a breeze genuinely does sit off its rest pose. It no longer
decides what the eye sees — at 40 % it had crept back to half of the tip's alternating power, all
of it below 0.4 Hz, because the lean is proportional to a wind magnitude whose envelope turns over
in five to fifteen seconds.

### The wind is a field, not a vector

`wind(strength, bearing, t)` returns one vector for the whole scene, which says a gust reaches every
limb in the same frame. `windAt(strength, bearing, t, position)` is that same gust sampled at a
**retarded time** — `t − (x·downwind)/2.5 m s⁻¹`, tilted by a cross-wind term so the front is not a
flat wall — plus a lateral offset into the noise lattice so an eddy has a finite width without
anyone having to write two-dimensional noise. `deform` and `flutterField` sample it per node, and
the same retardation shifts the turbulent forcing each node sees.

`windAt` at the origin is bit-for-bit `wind`, and shifting a bounded function's argument cannot take
it outside its range, so `maxWindMagnitude` and `maxWindSlope` carry over unchanged. They are
tested at positions all over the scene rather than only at the origin.

Two things are true about the crossing and they are not the same size. The **field** crosses
unambiguously: the gust at the most upwind leaf cluster leads the most downwind one by over a
second, and at any instant the wind across the crown varies by a third between its windiest and
calmest limb. The **motion** lags by about 0.2 s, not 0.9. A limb's displacement is mostly its
ancestors', and they sit nearer the trunk where the delays are smaller; what is left is a resonant
response at 1–5 Hz, where most of a second is most of a cycle and reads as a small lag. The field
buys decorrelation and a visible unevenness across the crown. It does not roll a wave of motion
across the tree, and no constant would make it — that would take a convection speed far slower than
any real gust on a crown this small.

The convection speed is deliberately not derived from `strength`. Gusts convect at roughly the mean
wind speed, so physically it should scale; `strength` is dimensionless and explicitly not a speed,
so scaling a metres-per-second constant by it would be inventing a wind speed and hiding it in an
exponent.

### Per-splat flutter

Everything above moves **nodes**. A node's transform is applied to every splat assigned to it, so
the ~55 splats of one leaf cluster travelled as a welded block — and a block of foliage that
translates rigidly is the most recognisable way for a tree in wind to look wrong. The rig cannot
express the fix: a leaf is not a node and will not be at any node count assignment can afford.

`packages/world/src/flutter.ts` adds a second, additive term, evaluated per splat:

```text
displaced = transform(canonical) + flutter(splatIndex, node, t)
```

- Amplitude falls as `exp(−radius / 15 mm)` with an exact-zero cut, so the trunk is _still_ rather
  than nearly still. The rule reads radius, not the band label, for the same reason `modes.ts`
  does: a mislabelled trunk that shimmered would be a far worse artifact than one that did not.
- Frequency is the node's own resonance clamped into 4–15 Hz. Splats of one node share it, so a
  cluster ripples rather than boils.
- Two orthogonal sinusoids at 0.7 and 0.3 of the amplitude, so `|offset| ≤ A` exactly and
  `maxFlutterAmplitude` is a bound rather than an estimate.
- No `Math.sin` per splat: `sin/cos(ω·t)` are per node per frame, and the per-splat phase is an
  angle addition against a 1024-entry table indexed by a hash of the splat index.

At the default wind a leaf splat swings 5.0 mm, which at 4–15 Hz is several times the per-frame
step its _node_ contributes, for a quarter of a reference splat radius of staleness. Cheap in
amplitude, rich in speed — the same trade S8 made when it moved the node model's energy up in
frequency without raising its reach.

Calm stays exactly identity: at zero wind every amplitude is exactly zero, the field reports itself
`still`, and `deformPositions` takes a branch that does not touch the coordinate at all — not even
to add zero, which would turn a `-0` into a `+0` and break the byte-identity the restore path and
`snapshot()` depend on.

### What that bought, and what it cost

| at the default wind, highest tip              | quasi-static (S7) | resonant modes (S8) | S9            |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------- | ------------- |
| `DEFAULT_WIND_STRENGTH`                       | 0.12              | 0.12                | **0.1**       |
| rig nodes / leaf clusters                     | 33 / 9            | 33 / 9              | **214 / 108** |
| splats                                        | 2,000             | 2,000               | **12,000**    |
| movement per frame, mean                      | 0.21 mm           | 1.6 mm              | **1.41 mm**   |
| _per splat_, mean, flutter included           | 0.21 mm           | 1.6 mm              | **1.80 mm**   |
| peak speed                                    | 0.04 m/s          | 0.28 m/s            | **0.36 m/s**  |
| dominant frequency                            | 0.3 Hz            | 1.1 Hz              | **1.26 Hz**   |
| alternating energy in 0.5–3 Hz                | ~0                | 64 %                | **65 %**      |
| alternating energy below 0.4 Hz               | ~100 %            | —                   | **22 %**      |
| alternating energy in 3.5–8 Hz                | ~0                | —                   | **11 %**      |
| peak splat displacement, measured over 60 s   | 10.9 cm           | 7.8 cm              | **7.7 cm**    |
| worst-case displacement **bound**             | 19.5 cm           | 19.7 cm             | **18.9 cm**   |
| sort staleness at the 2 cm reference gaussian | 9.7 radii         | 9.8 radii           | **9.5 radii** |

The two dashes are honest rather than decorative: S8 recorded a 0.5–3 Hz share and did not record
the two bands either side of it, and they are not recoverable now that the fixture it measured no
longer exists. The 22 % below 0.4 Hz is worth watching all the same, because that band is the
slow drift that made the quasi-static model read as a photograph — it was 38 % before the wind
became a field, and the field halved it without touching an amplitude.

The last three rows are the point, and they are the same point S8 made. Sort staleness is driven by
_amplitude_, not by speed, and `DEFAULT_WIND_STRENGTH` is derived from the staleness bound — so the
budget was held where it was, to within 3 %, and every gain was taken in structure and frequency.

**The constant itself moved, 0.12 → 0.1, and that is the derivation working rather than failing.**
The rig underneath it changed: 33 nodes became 214, every limb went from 5–15× too thick to
physically proportioned, and a tree with slender branches genuinely sways more per unit of wind.
Holding the derivation fixed and letting the constant move is what keeps the link between this
number and the artifact it guards. Holding the constant fixed would have spent nearly twice the
draw-order budget without anyone deciding to.

On a 6.3 m tree filling 800 px (127 px/m) that is 0.18 px per frame for a node and 0.23 px for a
splat, against 0.03 before S8. `packages/world/src/motion.test.ts`, `flutter.test.ts` and
`wind.test.ts` assert the properties — visibility with its viewing assumption written out, band
energy, sibling limbs decorrelated, per-splat decorrelation _within_ a single leaf cluster, a node
responding at its own frequency rather than the wind's, and a gust that arrives upwind first.

### What the fixture had to become first

The human who looked at S8 on a GPU said the tree moved but did not look realistic, and the
dominant confound was not the model. Slenderness — length ÷ diameter — in the fixture:

| limb   | fixture before | fixture now | a real tree |
| ------ | -------------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| trunk  | 2.0            | **15**      | 8–20        |
| branch | 4.8–6.8        | **24**      | 20–60       |
| twig   | 8.1            | **41**      | 50–200      |

Every limb was 5–15× too thick; a 1.15 m "branch" was 24 cm across. Since `ω ∝ radius / length²`,
those branches came out about ten times too stiff, landed above the forcing band and rode rigid, so
the trunk did all the visible work and the crown — where a tree's shimmer actually comes from — did
none. **The model was behaving correctly on a tree made of fence posts.**

Radii now follow da Vinci's rule exactly: a node's cross-section is the sum of its children's, plus
a per-segment taper. One tip radius therefore sets the whole tree's thickness, and that is also the
rule's limit here. A real 6 m tree carries thousands of twigs, this rig carries 108, and
`r_trunk = r_twig·√tips` means the two ends cannot both be realistic at 214 nodes. The tip radius is
chosen so the _trunk_ lands where a real trunk is, and the twigs come out near slenderness 41 rather
than a real twig's 50–200. Frequencies, which are what the motion model reads, land in the right
band at every level.

The geometry changed with the proportions: four generations instead of three, 108 leaf clusters
instead of 9, deterministic wobble on limb lengths and elevations so no two limbs of a whorl are
congruent, foliage as a sleeve of outward-facing leaf discs along each twig rather than an ellipsoid
blob past its tip, and bark that spirals along a limb with an azimuthal ripple. Splat count went
2,000 → 12,000, because 211 limbs cannot be drawn with three splats each.

## Wind is a scale, not a speed

`strength` is dimensionless, `0..1`, where 1 is the strongest gust this model produces. It is
deliberately not metres per second: calling it m/s would assert that the sway amplitude had been
validated against a real tree at that speed. It has not been, and no biomechanical source backs the
stiffness values or the frequency scale. Every surface that prints it says "an arbitrary scale, not
a wind speed".

`bearingDeg` is **downwind** — the direction the wind blows _towards_ — which is the opposite of the
meteorological convention. An inverted bearing is invisible in a screenshot, so the control's hint
says so in words.

Wind starts at 0 in every session and is never persisted (`state/settings.ts` is the only persisted
store): the first thing a person sees is the measurement, and motion is opted into again each time.
`settings.reducedMotion` forces calm and disables the control.

Turning wind on lands on `DEFAULT_WIND_STRENGTH = 0.1`, which lives in `@twin/world` beside the
bound it is derived from and is re-exported by `state/living.ts`. On the synthetic tree's rig that
is a worst-case displacement of 18.9 cm and 9.5 reference splat radii of draw-order staleness,
inside the largest strength whose staleness stays within one decade of the
`SORT_STALENESS_NOTICEABLE = 1` hypothesis (0.106). That hypothesis is not a measurement; see
below. The constant moved from 0.12 when the fixture's limbs stopped being posts — the derivation
is the fixed thing, not the number it produces.

## Limits

- **Single-tile splats only.** Snapshots aggregate over selected tiles, so splat indices are stable
  only while tile selection is. `splat_tiles.py` emits single-node tilesets, so indices are stable
  there. Anything multi-tile is refused, not approximated.
- **Draw order goes stale.** The splat sorter reads `primitive._positions` — canonical positions the
  deformer never touches — so a displaced splat carries a draw-order key for where it used to be.
  This is an artifact to measure, not a correctness bug, and `sortStaleness` in `@twin/world`
  measures it. It is also why **two frames with identical positions can differ pixel for pixel**:
  the sort runs asynchronously, so the same scene time can be drawn in two different orders. Both
  Living Survey e2e specs were first written comparing frames for equality, were flaky for exactly
  this reason, and had to be rewritten: they now assert byte-exactness where it is decidable — over
  the uploaded words, and through the deformer's own flags — and compare frames only for
  _inequality_.
- **The staleness yardstick is a reference, not this tileset.** `REFERENCE_GAUSSIAN_SCALE_M` is 2 cm,
  the median gaussian extent of a real drone capture. It is not the median of whichever tileset is on
  screen; the fixture's own median is 4.8 cm, so the printed figure overstates the fixture's
  staleness about 2.4-fold. Deriving the true one means decoding scale lanes out of the packed splat
  buffer, which nothing does today. The rule this imposes: **anywhere the ratio is printed, the
  yardstick is printed beside it.** The developer panel does, and it is the only place that shows
  it. A figure in "splat radii" with no stated denominator reads as measured and is not.
- **Splats are unpickable**, so the badge is ambient rather than attached to a selection, and the
  Inspector's Observed/Simulated section falls back to the active site (a click on a swaying tree
  produces a `ground` selection with no site at all). The section names the site it describes so it
  cannot be read as describing the clicked point.
- **The only tree that moves is synthetic.** `skeleton.py` has never been run on a real capture. Its
  scores are all against the synthetic tree, the one input where the answer is known: ARI 0.459
  against a ceiling of 0.799, purity 0.597 against 0.876, band agreement 0.601 against 0.989, mean
  joint offset 0.14 m, 99 % of true joints recovered within 0.5 m. The ceiling is what a _perfect_
  rig scores, because nearest-node assignment tops out at 87.6 % purity — a bark splat on the far
  side of a limb genuinely is nearer its neighbour's node, and three leaf sleeves on one fork
  genuinely overlap. Read the first figure against the second, not against perfection.
  Two of these moved a long way when the fixture became a real tree rather than a post with stubs,
  and in opposite directions. **Joint localisation got much better** — 0.14 m against 0.35 m, and
  99 % of true joints within half a metre against 70 % — because the truth now has 214 joints spread
  through the crown instead of 33. **Cluster agreement got worse** — ARI 0.459 against 0.672 —
  because assigning a leaf to one of 108 clusters 20 cm apart is a far harder question than one of
  9 clusters a metre apart. The second is the honest number to quote about a real capture, and it is
  the one that fell.
- **Stiffness is invented.** A linear gradient in thickness between the thinnest twig and the trunk
  base — thicker bends less — and nothing about it is calibrated. The one structural claim it now
  makes is that a joint's bend scales with the length of limb it stands for, so a rig's stiffness is
  a property of the tree and not of how finely the extractor sampled it.
- **So is the frequency scale.** `radius / length²` is beam theory; the constant that turns it into
  hertz was fitted so a 6 m trunk lands near 1 Hz, which is where real trees that size are measured.
  No species, no modulus, no measurement of any particular tree. It was refitted in S9 — 220 → 450 —
  because the old value had been fitted against a fixture whose trunk was a fence post, which is a
  reminder that a calibration constant is only as good as the thing it was calibrated against. The
  same goes for the damping ratios, for the shape of the turbulence spectrum (a `f^-1/2` amplitude
  falloff is in the right family for a wind spectrum and is not one), for the flutter amplitude and
  band, and for the gust convection speed.
- **The bound is looser than it was, in a way that flatters nothing.** `maxNodeAngle` is the sum of
  a saturated steady lean and a saturated oscillation, and the oscillation's worst case assumes all
  nine forcing sinusoids peak at once, over an ancestor chain that is now thirteen joints deep
  rather than four. The forcing frequencies are mutually irrational, so by Weyl's theorem that
  alignment does occur eventually and the bound is attained in the limit — it is a true supremum,
  not a pessimistic guess — but it sits about 2.5× the worst splat excursion actually seen over a
  minute (18.9 cm against 7.7 cm), where the quasi-static model's sat at 1.8×. The consequence is
  that `DEFAULT_WIND_STRENGTH` buys less typical amplitude for the same staleness budget than it
  used to, and the looseness grows with the rig's depth — a 500-node rig would be penalised again
  for being deeper without moving more. If that becomes the binding constraint, the answer is a
  _sampled_ peak displacement printed beside the proven one, not a weaker bound.
- **The fixture's twigs are still too thick, by about 20 %.** Da Vinci's rule ties the twig radius to
  the trunk radius through the number of tips, and 108 tips is two orders of magnitude short of a
  real tree's. Something had to give, and the trunk was chosen: it lands at slenderness 15 and
  1.33 Hz, both right, while the twigs come out at 41 where a real twig is 50–200. The frequencies
  land in the right band at every level, which is what the motion model reads, so this shows up as a
  fixture that is slightly stiffer than a real tree rather than as a model that is wrong.
- **The per-splat flutter is not a measurement of anything.** Its amplitude, its band and the
  thickness at which it dies away are three invented constants chosen so that a leaf cluster
  shimmers at millimetres rather than centimetres. What it is _not_ is a leaf: it is a small
  high-frequency offset that stands in for a degree of freedom the rig cannot hold, and two splats
  a millimetre apart on the same leaf get unrelated offsets where a real leaf would move as one.
- **Rigs are an explicit claim, and since A9 they are catalog data.** `renderConfig.rigUrl` on a
  splat asset names where that capture's rig sits, relative to its tileset URL; `rigUrlFor` in
  `apps/web/src/cesium/livingRigs.ts` resolves it. It used to be `LIVING_RIGS`, a hand-written
  table compiled into the web bundle, and the two properties that table protected both survive:
  nothing probes for `source/rig.json` (that would be a 404 per site per load), and "this site can
  move" is still a decision someone wrote down rather than a property of a file happening to exist.
  What it stopped costing is a frontend rebuild for every new capture.
- **ion-hosted assets can never be living**: the rig resolves relative to a tileset URL, and an ion
  asset has none.
- **Snapshots are pinned to the measured pose.** `CesiumSceneManager.snapshot()` holds calm for the
  captured frame, so the picture shows what was surveyed — a photograph of a swaying tree carries no
  label saying the sway was modelled.

## What a human on real hardware still has to settle

SwiftShader settles none of these. Headless frames here run 740–1730 ms, splat blending and
refinement do not look like they do on a GPU, and every screenshot in the e2e suite is evidence that
the pipeline runs end to end and nothing more.

1. **Does the sway read as _that_ tree?** The whole premise is motion that stays specific to the
   measured object rather than becoming a generic game-engine sway. That is a judgement made with
   eyes, at wind 0 → 0.1 → 1, and it has not been made. It has been attempted twice and failed
   twice, for different reasons. The first time the motion was 0.02 px per frame and the answer was
   arithmetic. The second time it was 0.14 px per frame, visible, and the answer was "moving, but
   not realistic" — and the reason was the fixture, whose limbs were 5–15× too thick, not the
   model. What S9 changed is almost entirely _structure_: the amplitude is where S8 left it and the
   crown now has 108 independently ringing clusters, per-splat shimmer and a gust that arrives.
   Whether that is enough is the third attempt, and it has not been made either.
2. **What does a frame actually cost?** 384 KB per frame for the fixture is arithmetic, and so is
   1.7 ms of CPU for its 12,000 splats and 5.0 ms for a hypothetical 150,000 (measured under Node,
   not in a browser). The real numbers are the upload, the re-sort it does not trigger, and whether
   the ladder's `setAnimating` behaviour keeps a mid-range laptop smooth with wind on.
3. **Where does the staleness threshold sit?** `SORT_STALENESS_NOTICEABLE = 1` is a hypothesis. The
   default wind strength was picked from it, so if the real threshold is much lower, 0.1 is too
   high; if much higher, the feature is being held back for nothing — and it is now being held back
   by a bound that sits 2.5× above the displacement actually reached.
4. **Does the crown read as foliage or as noise?** Per-splat flutter gives every splat of a leaf
   cluster its own offset. Splats of one node share a frequency so a cluster ripples rather than
   boils, but whether ~55 independently phased splats read as leaves in wind or as scintillation is
   exactly the kind of question SwiftShader cannot answer and a person can, in one glance.

Then the one that matters most: run `skeleton.py` on a real scanned tree and see whether a rig
inferred from geometry alone moves it convincingly. Everything above is demonstrable on a fixture
whose topology is known by construction, which is exactly the condition the extractor will not have
in the field.

### What would break on a rig that is not this one

Worth writing down, because the fixture is tidy in ways a real extraction will not be:

- **Nothing in items 1–4 reads a band label.** Natural frequency, damping, flutter amplitude and
  flutter frequency all come from radius and from the distance to the tips a node carries. `band`
  sets an angular limit and nothing else. That was already true of `modes.ts` and is now true of
  `flutter.ts`.
- **Node spacing no longer changes stiffness**, because a joint's bend is a curvature times its own
  segment length. An extractor that samples one limb finely and another coarsely gets the same tree.
  There is a cap (2 m) on how much limb one joint may be credited with, which is a guard against a
  rig too coarse to describe what it stands for, not a physical claim.
- **The assignment grid does not assume a tree.** It sorts nothing and prunes in all three axes, and
  it falls back to the double loop for a rig it cannot describe (one node, or coincident nodes).
  The earlier attempt — a slab along the longest axis — _did_ assume one, and was barely faster on
  the very shape it was designed for.
- **`skeleton.py` will not produce 108 leaf clusters from a real capture unless the crown is dense
  enough to resolve them.** `--max-nodes` is 200 now; on a sparse or noisy cloud the pruning will
  stop well short, and the crown will be coarser than this fixture's. Nothing breaks, but the
  shimmer moves up a level — fewer, larger clusters flutter as units.
- **`skeleton.py` measures the quantity `radius` means, but reports a bound for most of the
  crown.** It used to return `_rms_radius`, the horizontal spread of a cluster's _points_, which
  for a leaf cluster is the extent of the foliage and not the cross-section of the twig holding it
  up — on the fixture, true radii 8.8 mm to 15 cm against extracted 2.7 cm to 45 cm, the **median
  node on the 30 Hz clamp** and **9 of 189 nodes fluttering** instead of 180. `woody_radius`
  replaces it: a splat sits on the surface it represents and never inside it, so a limb is a
  hollow shell whose cross-section is a dense ring, while foliage is a diffuse halo about the same
  axis. Project a cluster across its limb, keep the ring, take its RMS radius. On the fixture the
  median recovered/true ratio is 1.18 against 5.56, and the crown comes out at a median 15.2 Hz
  with 75 nodes on the clamp and 164 fluttering — against 11.2 Hz, 58 and 173 for the same
  skeleton carrying the _true_ radii, which is the ceiling a 189-node skeleton of this tree can
  reach.
  **What it does not do is measure every node.** A cross-section with no ring in it — pure
  foliage, or several twigs a band welded into one cluster — is refused, and the node is given the
  cloud's resolution limit, `3 · spacing / 2π`, about half the point spacing. That is 130 of the
  fixture's 189 nodes, and a similar share on every tree tried. For those nodes the rig carries a
  bound that scales with **the capture's point density, not with the tree**. It is the right order
  of magnitude, which is all that was wrong before, and it is not a measurement. The estimator was
  checked across seven generated trees of different height, thickness, branching, foliage and
  density with no constant moved between them (median ratios 0.68–1.48, and two deliberate
  failures at the resolution limit); it has never been run on a real capture, because there is not
  one in this repository.
- **A zero-length segment is now a silent no-op.** Because a joint's bend is a curvature times its
  own segment length, a node sitting on top of its parent contributes nothing at all. The extracted
  rig has some (minimum segment 0.000 m). That is arguably right — a limb of no length has no bend
  — but under the previous rule such a node bent as much as any other, so the behaviour changed
  without anything failing.
- **A capture with foliage but no visible twigs** — which is most drone captures of a tree in leaf —
  gives an extractor no thin members to find, so every node will be thick, every node will be slow,
  and the crown will ride rigid again. That is the same failure this sprint fixed in the fixture,
  and it will return in the field wearing different clothes.
