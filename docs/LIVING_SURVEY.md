# The Living Survey

A measured site that moves under a wind setting without its measurement ever changing.

Today that is **one procedural tree**. The pipeline is real — a skeleton extractor scored against
ground truth, a motion model with proven bounds, a runtime that rewrites a Gaussian splat's
positions on the GPU every frame — but the only tree that moves is `data/tiles/synthetic-tree`, a
2,000-splat fixture generated by `tools/captures/synthetic_tree.py`. The skeleton extractor has never been run on a
real capture. What that would take, and why it did not happen here, is in
[CAPTURES.md](CAPTURES.md#the-real-tree-what-was-tried-and-what-it-would-take).

Read this page for the mechanism and its limits. The numbers below were all measured; where the
measurement was taken under software GL (SwiftShader), it says so, because those numbers are not
representative of a machine with a GPU.

### What this proves, and what it does not

The rest of this page is long, and the mechanism travels further than the caveats when it is
quoted. So both halves, up front:

**Proven, by measurement or by construction:**

- The canonical positions are never written to. A 1000-frame test asserts byte-identity on the
  engine's array, the fixture, the deformer's own copy and the cached bake matrix.
- Calm restores the measured pose **exactly**, not approximately — `bakeResidualM` measured 0
  against the real engine, so there is no epsilon anywhere in that claim.
- A splat tileset's positions can be rewritten every frame from outside the engine, with no fork
  and no readback, and the write lands in the frame it was made in.
- Motion cannot contaminate a measurement, by mechanism rather than by care.
- Idle stays idle: at calm the scene requests zero renders.

**Not proven, and not provable from here:**

- **That any of this works on a real scan.** The only tree that moves is invented. The skeleton
  extractor has been scored against ground truth but has never seen a capture.
- **That the sway reads as _that_ tree** rather than as generic foliage motion — the thing the
  Living Survey actually claims. No test here can answer it.
- **That the stale draw order is acceptable at a usable wind strength.** The threshold constant is
  a hypothesis, and `DEFAULT_WIND_STRENGTH` was derived from it rather than from observation.
- **What any of it costs on a GPU.** Every frame-rate figure here is software rasterisation.

The first three need a person, a real capture and real hardware. Until then this is a mechanism
that works on a tree nobody surveyed.

## Measured, simulated, fixture

The whole feature turns on a viewer never confusing these three, so they are named first.

| Thing                                    | What it is   | Where that is stated                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A real capture's splat positions         | Measured     | site provenance: capture date, GSD, license, attribution                |
| `data/tiles/synthetic-tree`              | **Fixture**  | "No capture date or resolution recorded" + the manifest's own words     |
| The skeleton rig (nodes, parents, radii) | Derived      | `rig.sourceNote`, printed verbatim in the Inspector                     |
| Per-node stiffness                       | **Invented** | a plausible gradient interpolated from constants; no biomechanics       |
| Wind `strength`                          | **Invented** | dimensionless 0..1, labelled "an arbitrary scale, not a wind speed"     |
| The sway itself                          | Simulated    | the ambient **Simulated motion** badge, plus the Inspector's Motion row |
| Displacement in metres, sort staleness   | Computed     | proven bounds over the rig, not samples                                 |

The Inspector splits one site into **Geometry** (what the catalog records: capture date, ground
sample distance, and the pipeline note verbatim) and **Motion** (Simulated, with the rig's source
note). "Measured capture" is earned, not assumed — it is said only when the catalog holds a date or
a resolution, and the synthetic tree holds neither, so it reads "No capture date or resolution
recorded · procedural; no capture, no reconstruction". The badge deliberately says nothing about
the geometry at all: it cannot see whether a given site was surveyed or invented, and for the only
tree that currently moves the answer is "invented".

Nothing about the motion can contaminate a measurement, and that is mechanism rather than care:
Gaussian splats never write depth and the splat primitive early-returns on the pick pass, so
`scene.sampleHeight` does not see them (measured: `undefined` with the globe hidden), a click
passes straight through a swaying tree to the terrain behind it, and no measurement tool can pick
one. The same property is why the badge has to be ambient — there is no hover or selection to hang
a disclosure off.

## Canonical and ephemeral, as mechanism

The product principle is that a living world never drifts away from its survey. That is enforced
by the shape of the code, not by a comment asserting it.

At attach, `SplatDeformer` decodes the baked positions once into buffers it owns and never writes
to again. Every tick computes

```text
displaced = f(canonical, t, wind)
```

from that pristine copy and uploads the result. There is no read-modify-write anywhere in the
path: the previous frame's output is never an input. So error cannot accumulate however long the
wind blows, and `strength = 0` does not "settle back" — it recomputes the canonical pose and
writes exactly that.

Two measurements make the claim exact rather than approximate:

- **`bakeResidualM` is 0.** The deformer re-bakes an undisplaced splat with the same term order
  `GaussianSplatPrimitive.transformTile` uses, and compares against the engine's own baked
  positions. Against the real engine in a browser (`apps/web/e2e/livingSurvey.spec.ts`) the largest
  disagreement over 2,000 splats is exactly zero, so wind → 0 restores the measured bytes with no
  epsilon in the claim. A residual above `BAKE_RESIDUAL_LIMIT_M` is a refusal, not a warning.
- **A 1000-frame immutability test.** `apps/web/src/__tests__/splatDeformer.test.ts` drives 1000
  frames of varying wind and then asserts byte-identity on `primitive._positions`, the harness's
  canonical array, the deformer's own canonical copy and the engine's cached bake matrix — and that
  `primitive._positions` is still the same object, not an equal one. After all that, zero wind
  lands on the engine's exact float32 bits again, word for word.

`tile.content.positions`, `primitive._positions`, `snapshot.positions` and the glTF POSITION array
are read-only to this feature, forever. `transformTile` rewrites the first of those in place from
the pristine glTF attribute, so a write there would make a deformation permanent and compound it
across every rebuild — the measured geometry would be destroyed silently, with nothing on screen
to show it. The only write targets are our staging buffer and the GPU texture.

## How it works

### The attribute texture

From `Shaders/PrimitiveGaussianSplatVS.glsl` (CesiumJS 1.145), each splat owns two adjacent
RGBA32UI texels on one row:

```glsl
ivec2 posCoord = ivec2(int((texIdx & rowMask) << 1), int(texIdx >> rowShift));
ivec2 covCoord = ivec2(int(((texIdx & rowMask) << 1) | 1u), int(texIdx >> rowShift));
```

The even column holds the position as raw float32 bits (`uintBitsToFloat`); the odd column beside
it holds packed covariance halves and an RGBA8 colour. Displacing a splat is writing three
float32s. Covariance and colour are never touched, which is what makes this safe without
reimplementing the engine's packer.

`rowMask` and `rowShift` are read from the primitive, never recomputed from an assumed maximum
texture size. They differ per machine — 8192 here under SwiftShader against 16384 on a typical
desktop GPU — and a wrong guess addresses every splat into the wrong texel while still producing a
picture.

### Why `CustomShader` is not the mechanism

`tileset.customShader` does not reach splat content in 1.145. Splats bypass the Model pipeline
entirely: `Scene/GaussianSplatPrimitive.js` builds its own render resources, its own
`PrimitiveGaussianSplatVS/FS` and its own DrawCommand, with zero `customShader` references. The
obvious route is closed. See [ADR 0006](DECISIONS/0006-splat-texture-rewrite.md).

### Mechanism (C′): interception, no patch, no readback

`Texture.copyFrom` takes a contiguous rectangle, and each position texel sits next to its
covariance texel, so any rectangle covering the positions covers the covariance too — writing rows
means supplying those bytes as well. Rather than reimplementing the packer (a lane-order mistake
corrupts geometry and colour silently) or reading the texture back (`gl.readPixels`, measured
762–898 ms for the first readback), `splatCapture.ts` keeps the buffer the engine already packed.

`generateSplatTexture` resolves `generateFromAttributes` as a property lookup on the
`GaussianSplatTextureGenerator` module object at call time, and that module is exported from the
public barrel (`cesium/Source/Cesium.js:638`). Replacing the property hands us the exact
`Uint32Array` the engine goes on to upload — correct by construction, and free. **No engine patch
was written**; `patches/@cesium__engine@26.3.0.patch` is the unrelated terrain-fill guard and is
untouched.

Cross-validated three ways on the `mygla` capture (57,410 splats): captured buffer → decoded
positions against `primitive._positions`, max absolute error **0.0 m**; GPU readback against
`_positions`, **0.0 m**; captured against readback, **0 differing words of 466,944**.

The interception only sees calls made after it is installed, so `CesiumSceneManager` installs it at
construction, before any tile loads. A snapshot it missed reports `no-capture` and the tree stays
still. `gl.readPixels` remains the recovery path for that case and is deliberately not implemented
on the steady-state path.

### The un-bake, and telling "re-derive" from "refuse"

`transformTile` writes `content.positions[i] = B · gltfPositions[i]`, where
`B = inverse(rootTransform) · tile.computedTransform · axisCorrection · content.worldTransform`,
and caches `B` on the content. Everything downstream is in that baked frame, while the rig is in
the local ENU frame the capture was authored in.

`rig.canonicalChecksum` is FNV-1a over the raw position bytes — **bit-exact, not tolerant** — and
was taken over the local positions, which `B` has since destroyed. The deformer therefore un-bakes
by `B⁻¹` before checksumming. That works because the capture pipeline snaps every coordinate to
SPZ's 1/4096 m grid before writing: `snap(B⁻¹ · B · p)` recovers the original float32 exactly as
long as the round trip's error stays well inside half a grid step, and it is around 2e-6 m for a
tree-sized capture against a 1.2e-4 m half-step. The synthetic tree's digest survives tiling,
loading and baking as `fnv1a32:2000:e272f8b5`, asserted in e2e.

This is also the discriminator the plan left open. `SiteManager.clampToGround` sets
`tileset.modelMatrix` after an asynchronous terrain sample, so every site rebuilds its snapshot
seconds after load with every baked position changed. A bit-exact checksum over the _baked_
positions could not tell that apart from the wrong tileset. Over the _un-baked_ positions it can:
same digest under a new `B` means clamp-to-ground moved the model, so the assignment is re-derived
from the new base (`rederivations` counts it); a different digest means these are not the splats
the rig was built for, and the deformer refuses. Rebuilds re-derive; they never re-apply the old
displacement.

### Refusals

Before its first write the deformer checks that the tileset is single-tile, that the root
transform's Z column is the geodetic normal (measured at 1.0 to 15 significant figures on a real
capture), that the tree's own points stand along local +Z, and that the checksum matches. On any
failure it writes nothing, ever, and reports which: `no-primitive`, `no-snapshot`, `no-texture`,
`no-bake-transform`, `no-capture`, `multi-tile`, `layout`, `frame`, `upright`, `checksum`, `bake`,
`internal`. A tree that does not move is honest; a tree whose wrong splats move is not.

### Per frame

`LivingSurveyManager` ticks on `scene.preUpdate` (which fires every tick even in
`requestRenderMode`, unlike `preRender`), computes node transforms with `deform()` from
`@twin/world`, and hands them to each attached deformer. Time is scene time —
`viewer.clock.currentTime` against a fixed epoch — so the same clock value always produces the same
frame, on any machine.

`scene.requestRender()` is called only while some deformer holds displaced positions, plus the one
frame that restores the measurement. At calm the deformer writes nothing at all, because
`markMovingNodes` compares against the identity transform _exactly_ and `@twin/world` guarantees
bit-exact identity at zero wind. An idle scene stays idle; a tolerance anywhere upstream would
quietly turn a still survey into a scene that renders forever.

Uploads cover only the rows the moving nodes occupy. For the 2,000-splat tree on this machine that
is one row of 8192 texels: 32,768 words, 128 KB per frame. (S0 measured whole-texture upload of
1.875 MB at ~1.2 ms CPU plus ~0.2 ms GPU submit — under SwiftShader, so treat it as an order of
magnitude, not a budget.)

### Animation and the quality ladder

`PerformanceManager` assumed a still camera meant a still scene: it only collected ladder evidence
while moving, and `sharpenAtRest` restored full device-pixel-ratio and MSAA 500 ms after the camera
stopped. With wind on, every animated frame would have rendered in the most expensive configuration
available, forever, while the ladder stayed blind to the cost.

`setAnimating(boolean)` fixes both. While animating, the still frame is not sharpened, and turning
it on drops an already-sharpened frame back to the ladder's step at once, the way the first frame of
a gesture does. Animated frames count against quality and never for it (`frameWeight`, pure and
tested): they feed the slow/good accounting that steps the ladder **down** — smooth ones included,
so one hitch a minute cannot ratchet quality away for good — but they never supply the credit that
steps it back **up**, and they are never the before/after sample that judges whether a step helped.
An animation can be smooth on a machine that cannot pan, and raising cost on the strength of frames
that are cheap because nothing is being asked of the machine is how an oscillation starts. An
animating scene can coarsen and earns its quality back on the next gesture. `animating` is surfaced
in the performance snapshot so the developer panel's `fps` is not read as something it is not. With
`setAnimating(false)` — the default — every prior test passes unmodified.

## Wind is a scale, not a speed

`strength` is dimensionless, `0..1`, where 1 is the strongest gust this model produces. It is
deliberately not metres per second: calling it m/s would assert that the sway amplitude had been
validated against a real tree at that speed. It has not been, and no biomechanical source backs the
stiffness values. Every surface that prints it says "an arbitrary scale, not a wind speed".

`bearingDeg` is **downwind** — the direction the wind blows _towards_ — which is the opposite of the
meteorological convention. An inverted bearing is invisible in a screenshot, so the control's hint
says so in words.

Wind starts at 0 in every session and is never persisted (`state/settings.ts` is the only persisted
store): the first thing a person sees is the measurement, and motion is opted into again each time.
`settings.reducedMotion` forces calm and disables the control.

Turning wind on lands on `DEFAULT_WIND_STRENGTH = 0.12`, chosen from the sort-staleness bound rather
than from a screenshot. On the synthetic tree's rig that is a worst-case displacement of 19.5 cm —
3.2 % of the tree's height, legible as motion at a glance — and 9.7 reference splat radii of
draw-order staleness, which is the largest strength whose staleness stays inside one decade of the
`SORT_STALENESS_NOTICEABLE = 1` hypothesis. That hypothesis is not a measurement; see below.

## Limits

- **Single-tile splats only.** Snapshots aggregate over selected tiles, so splat indices are stable
  only while tile selection is. `splat_tiles.py` emits single-node tilesets, so indices are stable
  there. Anything multi-tile is refused, not approximated.
- **Draw order goes stale.** The splat sorter reads `primitive._positions` — canonical positions the
  deformer never touches — so a displaced splat carries a draw-order key for where it used to be.
  This is an artifact to measure, not a correctness bug, and `sortStaleness` in `@twin/world`
  measures it. It is also why **two frames with identical positions can differ pixel for pixel**:
  the sort runs asynchronously, so the same scene time can be drawn in two different orders. Both
  Living Survey e2e specs were first written comparing frames for equality, were flaky for exactly
  this reason, and had to be rewritten: they now assert byte-exactness where it is decidable — over
  the uploaded words, and through the deformer's own flags — and compare frames only for
  _inequality_.
- **The staleness yardstick is a reference, not this tileset.** `REFERENCE_GAUSSIAN_SCALE_M` is 2 cm,
  the median gaussian extent of a real drone capture. It is not the median of whichever tileset is on
  screen; the fixture's own median is 10.7 cm, so the printed figure overstates the fixture's
  staleness about fivefold. Deriving the true one means decoding scale lanes out of the packed splat
  buffer, which nothing does today. The rule this imposes: **anywhere the ratio is printed, the
  yardstick is printed beside it.** The developer panel does, and it is the only place that shows
  it. A figure in "splat radii" with no stated denominator reads as measured and is not.
- **Splats are unpickable**, so the badge is ambient rather than attached to a selection, and the
  Inspector's Observed/Simulated section falls back to the active site (a click on a swaying tree
  produces a `ground` selection with no site at all). The section names the site it describes so it
  cannot be read as describing the clicked point.
- **The only tree that moves is synthetic.** `skeleton.py` has never been run on a real capture. Its
  scores are all against the synthetic tree, the one input where the answer is known: ARI 0.672
  against a ceiling of 0.847, band agreement 0.752 against 0.916, mean joint offset 0.35 m, 70 % of
  true joints recovered within 0.5 m. The ceiling is what a _perfect_ rig scores, because nearest-node
  assignment tops out at 90.5 % purity — a bark splat on the far side of a trunk genuinely is nearer
  its neighbour's node. Read the first column against the second, not against perfection.
- **Stiffness is invented.** A plausible gradient — thicker and lower bends less — interpolated from
  the synthetic generator's constants. Nothing about it is calibrated.
- **Rigs are an explicit table.** `LIVING_RIGS` in `apps/web/src/cesium/livingRigs.ts` names which
  capture slugs have a rig. Probing for `source/rig.json` would mean a 404 per site per load, and
  would make "this site can move" a property of a missing file rather than a decision someone made.
- **ion-hosted assets can never be living**: the rig resolves relative to a tileset URL, and an ion
  asset has none.
- **Snapshots are pinned to the measured pose.** `CesiumSceneManager.snapshot()` holds calm for the
  captured frame, so the picture shows what was surveyed — a photograph of a swaying tree carries no
  label saying the sway was modelled.

## What a human on real hardware still has to settle

SwiftShader settles none of these. Headless frames here run 740–1730 ms, splat blending and
refinement do not look like they do on a GPU, and every screenshot in the e2e suite is evidence that
the pipeline runs end to end and nothing more.

1. **Does the sway read as _that_ tree?** The whole premise is motion that stays specific to the
   measured object rather than becoming a generic game-engine sway. That is a judgement made with
   eyes, at wind 0 → 0.12 → 1, and it has not been made.
2. **What does a frame actually cost?** 128 KB per frame for the fixture is arithmetic; the real
   numbers are the upload, the re-sort it does not trigger, and whether the ladder's new
   `setAnimating` behaviour keeps a mid-range laptop smooth with wind on.
3. **Where does the staleness threshold sit?** `SORT_STALENESS_NOTICEABLE = 1` is a hypothesis. The
   default wind strength was picked from it, so if the real threshold is much lower, 0.12 is too
   high; if much higher, the feature is being held back for nothing.

Then the one that matters most: run `skeleton.py` on a real scanned tree and see whether a rig
inferred from geometry alone moves it convincingly. Everything above is demonstrable on a fixture
whose topology is known by construction, which is exactly the condition the extractor will not have
in the field.
